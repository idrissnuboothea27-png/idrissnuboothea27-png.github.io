About me.

A good message doesn't come from a string of unrelated marketing tasks.
It comes from a system: the right audience, the right channel, the right moment, and measurement behind it.

I'm Idriss Nuboothea, a marketing and communication officer based on Réunion Island. I help a marketing team turn what it sends into responses: campaigns customers actually read, contests people join, and studies that say what to change.

I started at Carrefour, in goods receiving and then at the checkout. That's where I understood that a promotion is only worth something if the customer sees it, understands it and responds to it. Since then, I have worked in trade marketing and in-store displays at Ferebam (GBH), then direct marketing, contests, events and customer research at Orange Réunion Mayotte. Rather than treating each channel separately, I connect them.

Many companies already have a customer base, social media accounts and campaigns. But these pieces often work in isolation: too many messages, customers who stop opening them, results nobody can read.

My role is to hold the whole chain from start to finish, from brief to segmentation, from copywriting to set-up, from sending to reporting, so the team gains clarity, consistency and results.

My goal is simple: design messages people read, send them at the right time, and measure how well they work from the results.

What I've learned

Did you knowmeasured in my own surveys

  • What drives a customer away isn't the number of messages, it's the pressure they feel.
  • A strong brand image doesn't protect against a badly dosed message.
  • Most sensitive profiles: under 38, mobile-only customers, reached by e-mail alone.
  • One customer in five doesn't remember any message received.
  • SMS remains the preferred channel: 43% of customers, versus 31% for e-mail.

My methodhow I go about it

  • Goal and audience first.
  • Channel and timing, then a short message.
  • Test, measure, adjust.
  • Document so that the team can do it again.

02

Customer survey on communication pressure

Orange Réunion, August 2026
Role
Questionnaire design, build on Eloquant, SPSS analysis, report and recommendations.
Problem
Dashboards measure sends and clicks, not how customers perceive commercial pressure or their avoidance behaviour.
Approach
26 questions on a 1-to-10 scale, five blocks (profile, frequency, perception, usage, image), short and accessible questions, sent by SMS to 12,000 customers.
Results
Three more sensitive profiles in the sample (under 38, mobile-only, reached by e-mail alone) and an action plan: perceived-pressure indicators, frequency by profile, respect for the preferred channel.

Monthly Orange Quizzes

Réunion and Mayotte, 2026
Role
Monthly design, Eloquant set-up, style sheet, operating guide for the team. January to August 2026, one quiz per month and per territory.
Problem
A chain of repetitive tasks with a risk of error every time: question, prize, dates, rules, identification, closing messages.

Standardised structure (eight fixed identification questions, one game question, automatic branching for minors); only the theme, prize and visual change. Black and orange dark theme, readable on mobile.
